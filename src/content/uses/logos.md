## When this helps

A designer has sent six logo concepts, or you've made a dozen yourself. The team has opinions and the loudest one is winning. A poll gives everyone the same vote, and you can ask the people who will actually see the logo: customers, members or readers.

## Why pairs suit logos

Logos differ in small ways: a heavier letter, a rounder mark, a different colour. Those differences are easy to see when two logos sit side by side and hard to see in a grid of twelve. Each voter only ever compares two at a time, and the ones they pick move on to the next round.

## How a poll for logos works

1. Export each option as an image on the same background and at the same size, so nothing but the design differs.
2. Create a poll with a question such as "Which logo suits a garden shop?" Naming what the business is helps voters judge fit, not just looks.
3. Pick a top size. A top 3 shows the runners-up, which helps if you plan to combine ideas from two options.
4. Send the link. Voters type a name and start. They don't sign up.
5. Open the results. You see the overall ranking and each voter's top list, with their name, so you can tell what customers chose and what the team chose.

## Tips

- Show each logo where it will live, such as on a shop sign, an app icon or a business card, if that's how people will meet it. Keep the mockup the same for every option.
- Include the current logo if you have one. It tells you whether a new one is preferred at all.
- Run two polls if you want to compare groups: one link for the team, one for customers. You can duplicate a poll to get the second link.
- Remove options you would never use. Every option adds picks for each voter.

## What a poll can't decide

A poll measures first impressions. A logo also has to work at small sizes, in one colour and next to your name, and people get used to a logo over time. Use the votes to narrow the shortlist and spot clear favourites, then make the final call with your designer.

## Common questions

### Can I see who voted for which logo?

Yes. You see each voter's name, when they voted and their top list. Voters see only their own list at the end. You can also turn on a read-only results link to show the outcome to others, without voter names unless you choose to show them.

### How many logos can I compare?

From 2 to 16 in one poll. With many options, consider cutting the list first, so voters spend their picks on real contenders.

### Do voters need an account?

No. They open the link, type a name and start. Only the person who creates the poll signs in, with a one-time link sent by email.

### Can someone vote twice?

Each browser gets one run per poll, and you can delete any entry you don't trust. It's a best-effort limit, so it suits polls among people you know.
