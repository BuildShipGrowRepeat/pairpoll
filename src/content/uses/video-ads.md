## When this helps

You have several video ads and budget to run two or three. Maybe they open with different hooks, or each one sells a different benefit. Paying to run every version is the slow way to find the weak ones. A poll with your own customers or community can cut the list first, so the ad budget goes to the strongest few.

## How a poll for video ads works

1. Export each ad as MP4 or WebM, up to 30 MB each.
2. Create a video poll with a viewer's question, such as "Which ad would you keep watching?"
3. Turn on "each video plays once".
4. Choose a top 1 to find the winner, or a top 3 if you plan to run three.
5. Send the link to people who look like your buyers. Voters type a name and start, without signing up.
6. Open the results. You see the overall ranking and each voter's top list.

## What "plays once" changes

With "each video plays once" on, both ads in a pair play to the end before the vote buttons appear. Voters can't replay, pause or skip ahead, so they judge each ad as it plays rather than studying it. Without it, a voter can rewatch the end of one ad and judge details a scrolling viewer never notices.

"Plays once" applies to each pair. An ad that wins moves on to its next pair and plays again there, so a voter may watch a strong ad several times before their top list is settled.

## What it doesn't do

A poll shows which ad people prefer when they watch on purpose. It doesn't track attention or eye movement, and it has no live delivery data such as views, clicks or cost per result. Ad platforms test that on real traffic: Google Ads can run a [video experiment](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10436762) that tests different video ads with the same audience, and Meta has [A/B tests for its ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1738164643098669). Use the poll to choose the finalists, then test those live.

## Tips

- Match length and format. Compare 15-second vertical ads with 15-second vertical ads.
- To test hooks, export only the first few seconds of each version. Voters then judge the part that differs, and the poll is quicker.
- Keep the poll short. Each pick means watching two ads to the end. A voter needs one pick fewer than the number of ads to find their winner, so six 20-second ads with a top 1 take five pairs, a little over three minutes of watching.
- Ask people who don't know the ads. Your team has seen every cut.

## Common questions

### Which files work?

MP4 or WebM, up to 30 MB each. One poll holds either videos or images. For still ads, see [ad creatives](/for/ad-creatives).

### Does it work on phones?

Yes. Videos start when the voter taps, with sound available, and the voter page works on phones.

### How many people should vote?

A handful of people can tell you which ads nobody picks. To separate two close favourites you need many more. [How many voters do you need?](/guides/how-many-voters-do-you-need) explains how to read small numbers.

### What if a video doesn't load?

The voter gets a retry, and a video that failed to load doesn't count as watched.
