Most thumbnail advice is a list of design rules. The rules help you make good options. They don't tell you which of your options to use. This guide is a process for that: make a few versions, narrow them down with your viewers, then let YouTube test the finalists.

## What a thumbnail has to do

A thumbnail has one job: make the right viewer choose your video over the ones next to it. To do that it has to:

- read at small size, on a phone, in a list of other videos
- show one idea, not three
- match the title, so the two together make one promise

If a thumbnail needs explaining, it isn't doing its job.

## Step 1: make versions that differ in one thing

Make four to eight versions. Keep most of the design the same and change one thing at a time: the face, the text, the colour or the crop. If every version differs in everything, the winner can't tell you what worked, and you start from zero next time.

YouTube's [thumbnail requirements](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72431) suggest a 16:9 image at least 640 pixels wide, in a format such as JPG or PNG.

## Step 2: check them at real size

Look at each version on your phone, at the size it appears in the home feed and in search. Text that reads well on your monitor often turns into a smudge. Drop any version you can't read at a glance. This step costs nothing.

## Step 3: narrow the list with your viewers

You've seen every version too often to judge them fresh. Your viewers haven't. Ask them before you publish:

1. Put the versions in a poll and ask the question a viewer would ask: "Which video would you click?"
2. Send the link where your viewers already are: the community tab, a Discord server, a newsletter.
3. Look at what people put first, and drop the versions nobody picks.

A vote in pairs suits thumbnails, because a viewer on YouTube also makes a quick choice between your video and the ones around it. [Let your viewers pick the thumbnail](/for/thumbnails) shows how a Pairpoll poll works for this.

Ask people who watch your kind of videos. Friends who don't will judge by different rules. A few voters can tell you which versions to drop. To separate two close favourites you need many more: [How many voters do you need?](/guides/how-many-voters-do-you-need) explains why.

## Step 4: test the finalists on YouTube

YouTube can [A/B test titles and thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400) on real viewers. You can test up to 3 thumbnails, and YouTube picks the winner by watch time share, not by click rate. Some conditions apply:

- It's in YouTube Studio on a computer, and your channel needs advanced features turned on.
- It doesn't work for Shorts, made-for-kids videos or private videos.
- A test should finish within two weeks, and it can take a few days, depending on how many impressions the video gets.

A viewer poll and YouTube's test answer different questions. The poll asks what people prefer when they look on purpose. YouTube's test measures what viewers do. [Preference testing vs A/B testing](/guides/preference-testing-vs-ab-testing) explains the difference. Use the poll to cut eight versions to two or three, and YouTube's test to choose between those.

## Step 5: keep notes for the next video

Write down what won and what the winner changed: a face instead of an object, fewer words, a brighter background. After a few videos you'll see which changes work for your channel and which don't, and you'll make better first versions.

If YouTube's test says the options performed about the same, it found no clear winner. Choose the version you prefer.
